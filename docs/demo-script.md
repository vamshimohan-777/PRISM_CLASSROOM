# Demo walkthrough (about 5 minutes)

All learners are simulated. Press **Reset** in the top bar before starting,
so every viewer sees the same data.

## 1. Frame the problem (30 s)

Open **About PRISM** on the sign-in page. One class, four learners, one topic
(Class VIII equivalent fractions). The question is whether one adaptive loop
works end to end with the teacher in charge.

## 2. Four learners, four routes (2 min)

Use the account switcher in the top bar.

| Account | What to show | Point to make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aarav | Fraction-bar comparator | Two answers carry the same "bigger denominator means bigger fraction" tag, so PRISM suggests a visual route. One answer alone would not have done this. |
| Diya | Equal-parts workbench | An error on a prerequisite concept, with little correct work, sends her back to fraction parts. |
| Kabir | Multiplier grid | His concept answers are right and his errors are arithmetic slips. He gets guided practice and is *not* reported as having a misconception. |
| Meera | Recipe scaling challenge | A correct, independent transfer answer unlocks an extension task. |

## 3. Teacher stays in control (1.5 min)

Switch to **Ms. Ananya**.

- The **Classroom Evidence Pulse** lists each learner. Click one to see the
  answer trail and the reason for their route.
- **Suggested Small Groups** shows one draft plan per route. Edit a plan's
  time or activity, move a learner to another group, and reject one plan.
  Every plan starts as *Proposed*, and nothing reaches students until the
  teacher acts.
- **Print Intervention Briefing** produces a one-page plan for the lesson.

## 4. Low connectivity (45 s)

As a student, click **Online** to switch to offline mode. Answer a question,
which is saved on the device. Switch back online and press **Sync**. Then show
the teacher view: the answer is counted once.

## 5. Close (15 s)

Every recommendation comes from a short, readable rule list
(`docs/rules-engine.md`), and each claim above has a matching automated test
(`docs/evaluation.md`). Real-classroom accuracy is future work and needs
school permission.
